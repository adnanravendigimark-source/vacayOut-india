# VacayOut – Complete State Page & Destination Page Content and Design Requirements

We need to clearly separate the purpose, content and design of a **State Page** and a **Destination Page**.

Example journey:

**Explore India → Himachal Pradesh → Manali → Individual attractions and experiences**

The goal is to help travellers find the information they need without searching through scattered, repetitive sections.

All content must come from researched, verified information stored in the database. Do not use mock data, generic filler or unsupported claims.

---

# 1. STATE PAGE – HIMACHAL PRADESH

URL example: `/india/himachal-pradesh`

## Purpose

The state page should help someone understand Himachal Pradesh as a whole and decide which destination is right for their trip.

A visitor should be able to answer:
- What is Himachal Pradesh known for?
- Which places should I visit?
- Which destination suits my interests?
- How many days do I need?
- When should I visit?
- How do I reach the state?
- What activities can I experience?
- What are the most useful travel routes?
- Where can I find detailed destination guides and itineraries?

## A. Hero section

Use a large cinematic landscape image of Himachal Pradesh.

Display:
- Breadcrumb: India → Himachal Pradesh
- State name
- Short, informative introduction
- Region
- Best-known travel experiences
- Best travel seasons
- Suggested trip duration
- Primary CTA: Explore destinations
- Secondary CTA: Plan with AI

The introduction should explain what makes Himachal Pradesh worth visiting, rather than using generic promotional text.

## B. About Himachal Pradesh

Write a comprehensive, practical overview.

Research and include:
- Geography and major mountain regions
- Main tourist circuits and valleys
- Popular destinations and what makes each different
- Climate and seasonal differences
- Culture, traditions and festivals
- Local food and regional specialties
- Languages and useful communication information
- What international and first-time travellers should know
- General travel considerations, permits and restrictions where applicable

Include useful arrival information:
- Main airports and their distances from relevant destinations
- Major railway connections
- Important railway experiences, such as the Kalka–Shimla Railway
- Volvo and ordinary bus connections from major cities
- Main road routes and important travel connections

For example, explain how travellers can reach Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala and Dalhousie from Delhi and Chandigarh. Do not simply list transport options; explain which route is practical for which destination.

# 3. HOW BOTH PAGES MUST CONNECT

The State Page and Destination Page should share the same database relationships.

Example:

**Himachal Pradesh**
- Manali
  - Hadimba Devi Temple
  - Solang Valley
  - Rohtang Pass
  - Atal Tunnel
  - Vashisht
  - Jogini Falls
  - Naggar
- Shimla
- Dharamshala
- Bir Billing
- Dalhousie

A place should have one primary destination and state relationship. Activities, guides and itineraries should connect to the relevant destinations and attractions.

When a visitor clicks Manali from the Himachal state page, they should land on the Manali destination page.

When they click a Manali attraction, they should land on its individual attraction page.

When they click a Manali itinerary, they should land on its dedicated itinerary page.

When they click a Manali travel guide, they should land on its dedicated guide page.

All these pages must link back to the correct destination and state.

# 4. DESIGN AND USER EXPERIENCE RULES

The pages should feel like one connected travel platform.

- Use a premium, clean editorial travel design.
- Keep the main content easy to scan.
- Use strong photography from the existing Vercel Blob image storage.
- Use clear section headings and short introductory explanations.
- Use maps where they genuinely help travellers understand locations and routes.
- Use cards for destinations, attractions, experiences, guides and itineraries.
- Use filters only where they help users find information.
- Avoid excessive accordions, repeated text and long unstructured paragraphs.
- Keep important practical information easy to find.
- Use consistent navigation and breadcrumbs.
- Make all sections responsive on mobile.
- Add contextual internal links between related pages.
- Never display empty sections or fake counts.
- Do not repeat the entire state guide on every destination page.

# 5. RESEARCH AND DATABASE RULES

Before implementation:

1. Inspect the current database schema and existing content.
2. Identify which information already exists and what is missing.
3. Research using official tourism websites, government transport sources, district administrations, attraction authorities, UNESCO and other reliable sources.
4. Store source URLs, publication dates and last-verified dates.
5. Store image credits and licence information.
6. Use the existing Vercel Blob image storage for researched images. Do not introduce another image storage system.
7. Keep verified facts, estimates, seasonal guidance and general recommendations clearly distinguished.
8. Never invent prices, opening hours, permits, distances, ratings, schedules or booking availability.
9. Keep content connected through proper database relationships, not hardcoded frontend arrays.
10. Preserve the existing admin review and publishing workflow.

First audit the existing Manali and Himachal Pradesh records. Identify missing information, duplicate content, incorrect relationships and sections that are not useful to travellers.

Then present the proposed data structure and content improvements before making destructive changes.

The final result should make VacayOut useful to a traveller from the moment they select a state until they finish planning their trip to an individual destination.
